# Reviewing the guides (about 20 minutes each)

All 15 guides are written as evergreen explanations: they state **methods and where to check**, not fees or fares that
go out of date. They are installed as **Pending review**. Two (`how-to-find-cheap-flights`, `best-time-to-book-flights`)
are live; the other 13 are drafts. Nothing is marked "Reviewed" for you, because that label is shown to readers as
*"Reviewed by a person on <date>"* and must be true.

## How to review one
1. Admin -> **Posts & Guides** -> filter **Not yet reviewed** -> **Edit**.
2. Read the whole guide. Open the sources listed below and confirm each point (or edit the text).
3. Add your own knowledge where you have it: a real example, a route you know, a mistake to avoid. Do not invent experiences.
4. Tick all 9 checklist items **only if each is true**, set *Review status* to **Reviewed**, *Visibility* to **Published**, save.
   (The server refuses to publish otherwise.) Add a short "what changed" note: it is kept in the revision history.
5. Optional but helpful: set a real author under Users & Backups -> Authors, and choose it in the guide.

## Raising the quality bar (recommended before applying)
Ad networks reward guides that clearly come from someone with real experience. For each guide, one or two paragraphs of **your own**
(a booking you actually made, a comparison you actually ran, with dates) will do more than extra length. Aim for roughly 800+ words
on your best 8-10 guides rather than publishing all 15 at the minimum.
